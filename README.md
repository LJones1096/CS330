## CS 330 Computational Graphics and Visualization

## How do I approach designing software?

Working on my 3D scene helped me understand how important it is to plan a project before trying to build everything at once. For my project, I created a retro arcade scene using basic 3D shapes and then added textures, lighting, and camera movement as the course progressed. One design skill I developed was learning how to break a complicated object into smaller shapes. The arcade cabinet was made from boxes, cylinders, and spheres that were positioned and scaled to create the final object. I can use this same approach in future projects by breaking larger problems into smaller parts that are easier to design, build, and test.

## How do I approach developing programs?

My development approach throughout this project was very iterative. I added features in smaller steps and tested the program frequently instead of making many changes at once. During the milestones, I worked with transformations, camera controls, textures, materials, and lighting. Testing each feature helped me find problems with positioning, lighting, and movement before continuing. I also learned that small changes to values such as position, scale, rotation, and lighting can have a major effect on the final result. This process showed me the value of testing often and making adjustments based on what I see when the program runs.

## How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?

Computational graphics gave me more experience with C++, debugging, problem solving, and working with a larger project containing multiple files and components. I also learned more about how mathematics is used in programming through transformations, coordinates, camera movement, and lighting. These skills can help me in future computer science courses because I have more experience working through problems that do not always have an immediate solution. Professionally, the programming and debugging skills from this course can apply to many areas of software development, even if I am not specifically working with computer graphics.
